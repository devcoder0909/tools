## The Computer Science of JPEG Compression: DCT & Quantization Matrices

The JPEG standard (standardized under **ISO/IEC 10918-1**) achieves massive file size reductions by exploiting the biological limitations of human vision:

### 1. RGB to YCbCr Color Space Conversion & Chrominance Subsampling
Natural images are captured in RGB (Red, Green, Blue). The JPEG compression pipeline immediately converts RGB coordinates into the **YCbCr color space**:
- **$Y$ (Luminance):** The grayscale brightness of the image.
- **$Cb$ (Chroma Blue) & $Cr$ (Chroma Red):** The color nuance channels.

Because human eyes contain approximately 120 million luminance-sensitive rod cells and only 6 million color-sensitive cone cells, human perception is overwhelmingly sensitive to brightness edges and relatively insensitive to fine color variations. JPEG applies **YUV 4:2:0 chrominance subsampling**, discarding $75\%$ of the raw color data without noticeable perceptual degradation.

### 2. The $8 \times 8$ Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT)
The image is segmented into non-overlapping blocks of $8 \times 8$ pixels. Each block is mapped from the spatial domain into the frequency domain using the forward Discrete Cosine Transform:

$$F(u,v) = \frac{1}{4} C(u) C(v) \sum_{x=0}^{7} \sum_{y=0}^{7} f(x,y) \cos\left[\frac{(2x+1)u\pi}{16}\right] \cos\left[\frac{(2y+1)v\pi}{16}\right]$$

This mathematical transformation concentrates the essential visual information into the top-left low-frequency coefficients (representing broad shading), while high-frequency noise and subtle texture details congregate in the bottom-right coefficients.

### 3. Quantization and Entropy Encoding (Huffman Coding)
Each frequency coefficient is divided by a value from a standardized **Quantization Matrix** ($Q$) and rounded to the nearest integer:

$$F_Q(u,v) = \text{round}\left( \frac{F(u,v)}{Q(u,v)} \right)$$

When you set the quality slider to $80\%$ or $85\%$, the high-frequency coefficients round off to zeros. A zigzag scanning order groups long sequences of consecutive zeros, which are compressed losslessly using Run-Length Encoding (RLE) and Huffman entropy coding.

### 4. Baseline Sequential vs Progressive JPEG
Standard JPEG images encode data in a **baseline sequential format**, rendering the image line-by-line from top to bottom as data streams across the network. 

In contrast, **Progressive JPEG** organizes coefficients into multiple structural scans:
- **Scan 1 (DC Coefficients):** Displays an immediate, low-resolution rough preview of the entire image across the screen in milliseconds.
- **Scans 2 to 5 (AC Coefficients):** Incrementally refines high-frequency details, sharpness, and textures until the complete high-resolution picture is fully displayed.
- **Compression Efficiency Advantage:** Progressive JPEGs typically achieve **2% to 8% smaller file sizes** than baseline JPEGs because grouping similar frequency coefficients across all blocks enhances Huffman entropy compression.

### Does compressing a JPG multiple times degrade quality (generational loss)?
Yes. Because JPEG uses lossy DCT compression, re-encoding an already compressed JPEG introduces cumulative rounding errors (generational loss), creating visible blockiness and mosquito noise around high-contrast edges. Always compress from the original uncompressed or high-quality master photograph.

### Can I batch compress multiple JPG photos at the same time?
Yes. You can select multiple images or drag an entire folder into ZixTool. Each image is compressed concurrently in local RAM, and you can download all compressed files individually or packaged together in a single ZIP archive.

### Does ZixTool strip private EXIF camera metadata?
Yes. When re-encoding images, our engine automatically strips sensitive EXIF metadata—including GPS geolocation coordinates, camera serial numbers, and capture timestamps—protecting your personal privacy when sharing photos online.

### Why is my JPG file larger than expected after converting from PNG?
If the original PNG contains flat vector logos, solid color backgrounds, or simple line art, PNG's lossless Deflate algorithm is more efficient than JPEG. For photographs and continuous-tone scenes, however, JPEG will almost always achieve much smaller file sizes.

### Can I use this JPG compressor offline without internet access?
