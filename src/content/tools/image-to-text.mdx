## Copy text from a screenshot

Choose a JPG, PNG or WebP that shows the words you need. Pick the language printed in the picture and select **Extract text**. When the result appears, click inside it to correct a word or remove an extra line break. Select **Copy text** to paste the edited result into a document, message or spreadsheet.

For example, if a timetable arrives as a screenshot, crop the station name and departure time together. Compare each digit against the original before pasting it into your itinerary. A narrow crop can also keep text from an adjacent column out of the result.

## Choose the language of the image

The language menu controls recognition. For a scanned Hindi notice, select हिन्दी even when your browser menus are in English. Arabic results support right-to-left display. Recognition produces text in the original language. For a bilingual poster, crop the two sections and run each with its own language setting.

## Improve a difficult result

Start with a straight, well-lit image. Tilted pages, tiny lettering, glare and text laid over a patterned background make recognition harder. Use the [image cropper](/crop-image/) to remove unrelated pictures and margins, then try the original-resolution crop. Compare similar characters such as zero and the letter O, and check accents, punctuation and names manually.

The output is editable plain text. Multi-column layouts and tables may need rearranging after extraction. Use the [word counter](/word-counter/) to measure the corrected passage once its reading order looks right.

## How the image is processed

Your browser downloads the recognition engine and selected language data from ZixTool. The image itself is processed on your device. A larger photo can take longer or use more memory, especially on a phone. If a full-page image fails, extract a paragraph at a time and copy each result before selecting the next file.
