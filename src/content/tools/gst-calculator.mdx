## The Mathematics of GST: Exclusive vs Inclusive Tax Formulas

Goods and Services Tax calculations depend on whether tax is being added to a base cost or extracted from an all-inclusive retail price:

### 1. Adding Tax: GST Exclusive Mode Formula
When a business quotes a net price before tax, the GST amount and gross total are computed as follows:

$$\text{GST Amount} = \text{Net Base Price} \times \left( \frac{\text{GST Rate}}{100} \right)$$

$$\text{Gross Invoice Total} = \text{Net Base Price} + \text{GST Amount} = \text{Net Base Price} \times \left( 1 + \frac{\text{GST Rate}}{100} \right)$$

*Worked Example:* A software consulting service quotes a net base fee of $\$1,000.00$ subject to an $18\%$ GST rate:
- GST Payable: $\$1,000.00 \times 0.18 = \mathbf{\$180.00}$
- Gross Total: $\$1,000.00 + \$180.00 = \mathbf{\$1,180.00}$
- Intra-State Split: $\text{CGST } (9\%) = \mathbf{\$90.00}$, $\text{SGST } (9\%) = \mathbf{\$90.00}$

### 2. Removing Tax: GST Inclusive Mode (Reverse Tax Extraction)
When a consumer purchase includes embedded tax (common in retail point-of-sale pricing), naive shoppers mistakenly calculate tax by multiplying the gross total by the tax rate. On a $\$118.00$ gross purchase, $18\%$ of $\$118.00$ is $\$21.24$, which incorrectly results in a $\$96.76$ base price.

The mathematically correct reverse extraction formula divides by the compounding tax factor:

$$\text{Net Base Price} = \frac{\text{Gross Total Amount}}{1 + \left( \frac{\text{GST Rate}}{100} \right)}$$

$$\text{Embedded GST Amount} = \text{Gross Total Amount} - \text{Net Base Price}$$

*Worked Example:* Extracting $18\%$ embedded GST from a $\$1,180.00$ gross total:
$$\text{Net Base Price} = \frac{\$1,180.00}{1 + 0.18} = \frac{\$1,180.00}{1.18} = \mathbf{\$1,000.00}$$
$$\text{Embedded GST} = \$1,180.00 - \$1,000.00 = \mathbf{\$180.00}$$

## Understanding CGST, SGST, UTGST, and IGST Tax Splits

Under dual-GST fiscal frameworks (such as in India), taxation is shared between the national federal government and regional states:

### 1. Intra-State Supplies (Within the Same State)
When the supplier's location and the place of supply reside within the same state:
- **CGST (Central Goods and Services Tax):** Exactly $50\%$ of the total GST rate collected by the Central Government.
- **SGST (State Goods and Services Tax):** Exactly $50\%$ of the total GST rate collected by the State Government.
- *Example:* On an $18\%$ transaction, $9\%$ is levied as CGST and $9\%$ is levied as SGST.

### 2. Intra-Union Territory Supplies
For Union Territories without an independent legislature (such as Chandigarh, Ladakh, or Lakshadweep), the state portion is collected as **UTGST (Union Territory Goods and Services Tax)** alongside CGST.

### 3. Inter-State Supplies (Between Different States)
When a transaction crosses state borders (e.g., a supplier in Maharashtra delivers goods to a client in Karnataka):
- **IGST (Integrated Goods and Services Tax):** Levied at the full tax rate ($100\%$ of the GST slab). The revenue is collected centrally and apportioned to the destination consumer state.

## Global GST and VAT Comparison: International Rates

Goods and Services Tax is a multi-stage consumption tax adopted across more than 160 countries worldwide under GST or Value-Added Tax (VAT) terminology:

- **Australia (GST):** A single flat rate of **$10\%$** applied to most goods, services, and imported items, with exemptions for basic food, medical care, and education.
- **Canada (GST / HST):** A federal GST of **$5\%$**, which is combined with provincial sales tax in participating provinces as Harmonized Sales Tax (HST) ranging from $13\%$ to $15\%$ (e.g., $13\%$ in Ontario, $15\%$ in Nova Scotia).
- **Singapore (GST):** A broad-based consumption tax of **$9\%$** (effective January 2024) levied on all domestic goods, services, and digital imports.
- **New Zealand (GST):** A comprehensive flat rate of **$15\%$** with minimal exemptions.
- **United Kingdom & EU (VAT):** Operates on the same value-added principle, with standard rates ranging from $19\%$ (Germany) to $20\%$ (UK, France) and $25\%$ (Sweden, Denmark).

## Input Tax Credit (ITC) for B2B Registered Entities

For businesses registered under GST, the tax paid on procurement of raw materials, capital equipment, and operational services is not an outright expense. Registered businesses can claim **Input Tax Credit (ITC)**:

$$\text{Net Tax Payable to Government} = \text{Output GST (Sales)} - \text{Input Tax Credit (Purchases)}$$

This cascading-tax prevention mechanism ensures that tax is paid strictly on the incremental value added at each manufacturing and distribution tier, preventing double taxation of end consumers.
