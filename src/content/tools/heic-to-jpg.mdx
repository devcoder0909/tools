## HEIC vs JPG: Technical Differences, Quality & Compatibility

Apple introduced the High Efficiency Image Container (HEIC) format in iOS 11 to replace standard JPEG as the default camera capture format. Understanding the differences helps you decide when to keep HEIC and when converting to JPG is essential.

### Compression Efficiency and Storage
HEIC relies on modern HEVC (H.265) video compression algorithms to compress still photographic data. As a direct result, an HEIC photograph requires roughly 40% to 50% less disk space than a comparable JPEG captured at identical optical fidelity. A 12-megapixel smartphone photo that consumes 4.2 megabytes as a standard JPG often occupies just 2.1 megabytes in HEIC format.

### Universal Compatibility Across Ecosystems
While HEIC is superior in byte efficiency, its primary drawback is fragmented operating system and software compatibility:
- **Windows Support:** Standard Windows 10 and 11 installations do not bundle native HEIC decoders out of the box. Users are routinely prompted to purchase paid video extensions from the Microsoft Store simply to preview photos in the Windows Photos app.
- **Web Browsers:** Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari do not support embedding raw `.heic` files in web pages (`&lt;img&gt;` tags) without custom decoding scripts.
- **Online Portals:** Government job portals, university application systems, banking verifications, and real estate portals almost universally reject HEIC uploads and mandate standard `.jpg` or `.png` files.

### Color Depth and Dynamic Range
HEIC supports 16-bit color channels and wide-gamut P3 color reproduction, capturing richer gradients during high dynamic range (HDR) landscape shots. In contrast, standard JPG is restricted to an 8-bit color depth (256 shades per RGB channel). When converting HEIC to JPG, dynamic ranges are tone-mapped to standard 8-bit sRGB color spaces, ensuring identical appearance on every television, smartphone screen, and office monitor worldwide.
