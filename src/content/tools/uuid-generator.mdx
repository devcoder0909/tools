## What is a Version 4 UUID?

A Universally Unique Identifier (UUID), defined by RFC 4122, is a 128-bit label formatted as 32 hexadecimal digits displayed in five groups separated by hyphens (`8-4-4-4-12`):

$$\text{xxxxxxxx-xxxx-4xxx-yxxx-xxxxxxxxxxxx}$$

Key architectural characteristics include:
- **Version 4 Bit Flag:** The 13th character is always `4`, denoting that the identifier was constructed from pseudorandom numbers rather than MAC hardware addresses or timestamps.
- **Variant 1 Flag:** The 17th character starts with `8`, `9`, `a`, or `b`, denoting compliance with standard DCE 1.1 / RFC 4122 variant conventions.
- **Collision Impossibility:** With $2^{122}$ (approximately $5.3 \times 10^{36}$) possible values, generating 1 billion UUIDs per second for 100 years yields a collision probability smaller than one in a billion.
