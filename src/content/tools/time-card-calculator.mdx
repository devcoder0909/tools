## How do I calculate hours worked with a lunch break?

Enter the times you started and finished work. In the break field, enter the minutes to deduct. If you took two unpaid breaks of 15 and 30 minutes, enter 45. Leave days you did not work blank.

Suppose you clock in at 8:45 AM and clock out at 5:10 PM. The shift spans 8 hours and 25 minutes. A 40-minute unpaid lunch brings the day's total to **7 hours and 45 minutes**, or **7.75 decimal hours**. Only enter a deduction for time you mean to exclude from your record.

## What does a weekly time card total look like?

| Shift | Clock in | Clock out | Unpaid break | Hours worked |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | 8:45 AM | 5:10 PM | 40 min | 7:45 |
| Tuesday | 9:00 AM | 5:00 PM | 30 min | 7:30 |
| Wednesday overnight | 10:00 PM | 6:00 AM next day | 30 min | 7:30 |
| Total | | | | 22:45 |

Those three shifts add up to **22.75 hours**. Add the underlying minutes before converting the weekly total to decimal hours. Rounding each day's decimal figure first can change the sum.

## Is 7 hours and 45 minutes the same as 7.45 hours?

Divide the minutes by 60 to get the decimal part. Fifteen minutes is 0.25 hours, 30 is 0.50 and 45 is 0.75. A time card showing **7:45** therefore corresponds to **7.75** in a spreadsheet that expects decimal hours. Entering 7.45 there would record 7 hours and 27 minutes.

## How should I record an overnight shift?

Use the row for the day the shift starts and select the next-day option. For example, a Sunday shift from 10 PM to 6 AM Monday spans eight clock hours. The whole shift belongs to the Sunday row in this calculator. If you need separate totals for two reporting periods, split the shift at the boundary and apply each break once.

The calculator uses clock times without a date or time zone. If clocks changed during the shift, check the actual elapsed time against a dated time record. To plan full working days across dates, use the [US working days calculator](/date-difference-calculator/#us-working-days).
