## The Mathematics of Aspect Ratio: Euclidean GCD Algorithm & Proportions

Aspect ratio defines the proportional relationship between a visual element's horizontal width and vertical height:

$$\text{Aspect Ratio} = \frac{\text{Width}}{\text{Height}} = X : Y$$

### 1. Simplifying Raw Pixels to Irreducible Ratios (Euclidean Algorithm)
To reduce an arbitrary pixel resolution (such as $2560 \times 1440$) down to an irreducible fraction like $16:9$, the calculation engine computes the **Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)** using Euclid's recursive algorithm:

$$\gcd(a, 0) = a, \quad \gcd(a, b) = \gcd(b, a \pmod b)$$

For $2560$ and $1440$:
- $\gcd(2560, 1440) = \gcd(1440, 1120)$
- $\gcd(1440, 1120) = \gcd(1120, 320)$
- $\gcd(1120, 320) = \gcd(320, 160)$
- $\gcd(320, 160) = 160$

Dividing each dimension by the GCD ($160$):
$$\text{Ratio Width} = \frac{2560}{160} = \mathbf{16}, \quad \text{Ratio Height} = \frac{1440}{160} = \mathbf{9} \implies \mathbf{16:9}$$

### 2. The Proportional Scaling Formulas
When resizing an asset to match a new container width ($W_{\text{new}}$) or height ($H_{\text{new}}$) without distortion:

$$H_{\text{new}} = W_{\text{new}} \times \left( \frac{H_{\text{original}}}{W_{\text{original}}} \right)$$

$$W_{\text{new}} = H_{\text{new}} \times \left( \frac{W_{\text{original}}}{H_{\text{original}}} \right)$$

*Worked Example:* You have a $4\text{K UHD}$ graphic ($3840 \times 2160$) and need to resize it to a website container width of $1200\text{px}$:
$$H_{\text{new}} = 1200 \times \left(\frac{2160}{3840}\right) = 1200 \times 0.5625 = \mathbf{675\text{px}}$$

## Visual Display Artifacts: Letterboxing, Pillarboxing, and Stretching

When video content or graphics are displayed on a screen with a non-matching aspect ratio, rendering systems employ three primary compensation techniques:

### 1. Letterboxing (Horizontal Black Bars)
When wide aspect ratio content (such as a $16:9$ or $21:9$ movie) is viewed on a narrower or squarer screen (such as an older $4:3$ television or a $16:10$ tablet), black horizontal bars are rendered above and below the image to preserve the entire horizontal field of view without cropping.

### 2. Pillarboxing (Vertical Black Bars)
When narrow aspect ratio content (such as a vintage $4:3$ television show or an old home video) is viewed on a modern $16:9$ widescreen display, black vertical bars appear on the left and right sides of the screen to prevent horizontal distortion.

### 3. Windowboxing (All Four Sides)
Occurs when $4:3$ content that was improperly formatted with hardcoded letterboxing is displayed on a $16:9$ display, resulting in black borders encircling all four edges of the video frame.

### 4. Non-Uniform Stretching (Geometric Distortion)
If software scales an image or video to fit a display container without maintaining aspect ratio lock, circular objects warp into ovals and human subjects appear unnaturally stretched or squashed. Utilizing proportional math preserves natural geometric symmetry.
