## Why Convert Heavy TIFF Scans to JPG: Resolving Storage & Compatibility Issues

Tagged Image File Format (TIFF) is the standard archival format created by Aldus and Adobe for flatbed scanners, medical imaging devices, and pre-press publishing. However, its uncompressed structure creates severe operational roadblocks:

- **Complete Lack of Web Browser Support:** Modern web browsers (Google Chrome, Apple Safari, Mozilla Firefox, Microsoft Edge) cannot display `.tiff` files natively inside standard `<img>` tags. Converting to standard JPG enables instant web viewing, email previews, and social sharing.
- **Enormous Storage Footprints (20MB to 100MB+ per Page):** Office scanners (Epson, Canon, Brother, HP, Xerox) frequently output uncompressed 300 DPI or 600 DPI TIFF scans that consume 30MB to 80MB per single page. Converting to JPEG slashes file sizes by 85% to 95% without compromising text legibility or seal clarity.
- **Government Portal & Email Attachment Rejections:** Tax submission portals, court filing systems, university admissions, and email providers (like Gmail and Outlook) reject TIFF files due to strict 20MB file attachment limits. Converting to JPG guarantees 100% acceptance.
- **Mobile Device Viewing Failures:** iPhone and Android default photo galleries and messaging apps cannot open raw TIFF files without downloading third-party viewer applications.
