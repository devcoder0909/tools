### Understanding the Official BMI Formula

Body Mass Index is defined as body mass divided by the square of body height:

- **Metric Formula:**
  $$BMI = \frac{\text{weight (kg)}}{\text{height (m)}^2}$$

- **Imperial Formula:**
  $$BMI = \frac{\text{weight (lbs)} \times 703}{\text{height (inches)}^2}$$

## World Health Organization (WHO) Weight Classifications

The table below outlines international adult weight categories established by the World Health Organization:

| BMI Range | Classification | Associated Health Risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Under 18.5** | Underweight | Nutritional deficiency, weakened immune function |
| **18.5 – 24.9** | Normal Weight | Lowest risk of cardiovascular and metabolic disorders |
| **25.0 – 29.9** | Overweight | Moderate risk of hypertension and type-2 diabetes |
| **30.0 – 34.9** | Obese (Class I) | Substantial risk of cardiovascular disease |
| **35.0 and Above** | Obese (Class II & III) | High clinical risk; physician consultation recommended |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is BMI accurate for athletes and bodybuilders?
BMI does not distinguish between lean muscle mass and adipose fat tissue. Muscular individuals may register as "overweight" while maintaining very low body fat.

### What is considered a healthy BMI range?
For adult men and women, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered the healthy baseline range.
