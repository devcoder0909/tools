## How to calculate percentage increase: the mathematical formula

Calculating the percent increase between two numbers follows a simple three-step mathematical process:

1. **Find the Absolute Increase:** Subtract the starting value (original number) from the final value (new number).
2. **Divide by the Starting Value:** Divide the increase by the original starting number.
3. **Multiply by 100:** Convert the decimal fraction into a standard percentage.

$$\text{Percentage Increase} = \left(\frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Original Value}}{\text{Original Value}}\right) \times 100$$

### Step-by-Step Worked Example:

Suppose an employee's hourly wage increases from $25.00 to $30.00:
- **Step 1 (Difference):** $\$30.00 - \$25.00 = \$5.00$
- **Step 2 (Division):** $\frac{\$5.00}{\$25.00} = 0.20$
- **Step 3 (Percentage):** $0.20 \times 100 = 20\%$

The employee received an exact **20% pay raise**.

## Percentage increase vs. percentage change vs. percentage difference

Understanding mathematical terminology prevents common calculation mistakes:

- **Percentage Increase:** Specifically measures positive growth where the final value exceeds the starting value ($\text{New} > \text{Old}$).
- **Percentage Change:** The general category encompassing both positive gains (percentage increase) and negative drops (percentage decrease). When the new value is smaller than the original, the result is negative.
- **Percentage Difference:** Used when comparing two positive values without an inherent chronological order or starting baseline. Instead of dividing by the first number, it divides by their average.
