## What is a reverse percentage?

A reverse percentage problem involves finding the starting number (the base or original value) when you only know the final outcome and the percentage change applied to it. 

People frequently make the mistake of calculating the percentage directly on the final price. For example, if a jacket on sale costs $80 after a 20% discount, adding 20% back to $80 gives $96 ($80 + $16), which is **incorrect**. The original price was actually $100, because 20% of $100 is $20 ($100 - $20 = $80). Because the original percentage was taken from a larger base number, finding the true starting amount requires the reverse percentage formula.

## The reverse percentage formulas

Depending on whether the final value resulted from a discount (decrease) or a markup/tax (increase), use the corresponding mathematical formula:

### 1. Reverse Percentage Formula for a Discount or Decrease

$$\text{Original Value} = \frac{\text{Final Value}}{1 - \left(\frac{\text{Percentage}}{100}\right)}$$

**Worked Example:** An electronics store sells a laptop for $425 after a 15% markdown.
- Percentage as decimal: $15\% = 0.15$
- Denominator: $1 - 0.15 = 0.85$
- Original price: $\frac{425}{0.85} = \$500.00$

### 2. Reverse Percentage Formula for an Increase or Sales Tax

$$\text{Original Value} = \frac{\text{Final Value}}{1 + \left(\frac{\text{Percentage}}{100}\right)}$$

**Worked Example:** A restaurant bill is $108.00 including an 8% sales tax.
- Percentage as decimal: $8\% = 0.08$
- Denominator: $1 + 0.08 = 1.08$
- Pre-tax subtotal: $\frac{108}{1.08} = \$100.00$
