## Why Do PDF Files Become So Large?

Understanding what bloats a PDF helps you achieve maximum compression without sacrificing document quality:

1. **Embedded High-Resolution Images:** A single photo or scanned page captured at 600 DPI can consume 8 MB to 15 MB. A 10-page scan quickly reaches 100 MB.
2. **Embedded Full Font Subsets:** When you export a document from desktop publishing software, entire font packages (TrueType / OpenType) are frequently embedded, adding hundreds of kilobytes per font family.
3. **Uncompressed Cross-Reference (XRef) Streams:** Older PDF specifications (PDF 1.4 or earlier) store object cross-reference tables in uncompressed ASCII format rather than modern compressed binary streams.
4. **Redundant XML Metadata & Edit History:** Archival PDFs generated by Adobe Acrobat or enterprise scanners often store revision history, thumbnails, and color management profiles that are unnecessary for viewing or printing.

## Frequently Encountered Issues & Troubleshooting

### Why did my PDF only shrink by 5% to 10%?
If your PDF consists entirely of digital vector text (such as an academic article or eBook with no images), standard Deflate compression has already been applied. In such documents, there are few or no image pixels to resample.

### Will my PDF still look good when printed?
Yes. Our recommended compression profile maintains 150 DPI resolution for photographic elements, which is visually crisp on both high-DPI desktop monitors and standard laser printers.

### Can I compress an encrypted or password-protected PDF?
Encrypted PDFs restrict object stream re-encoding. You must enter the document password to decrypt the file before running compression.
