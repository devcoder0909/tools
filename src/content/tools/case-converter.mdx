## Complete Guide to Supported Text Case Formats

Understanding when and where to employ specific casing styles ensures clarity, adherence to style guides, and syntactically valid code:

### 1. Literary and Editorial Casing Styles
- **UPPERCASE (ALL CAPS):** Converts every alphabetical letter to its capitalized variant (`SAMPLE TEXT`). Used primarily for legal disclaimers, urgent warnings, acronyms (NASA, UNESCO), and SQL query keywords (`SELECT`, `WHERE`, `JOIN`).
- **lowercase:** Converts all letters to standard small characters (`sample text`). Useful for sanitizing search queries, normalizing raw text data for natural language processing (NLP), or resetting accidental Caps Lock typing.
- **Title Case:** Capitalizes the initial letter of major lexical words while keeping grammatical articles, short prepositions, and conjunctions in lowercase (`The Quick Brown Fox Jumps Over the Lazy Dog`). Indispensable for book titles, blog headlines, and academic paper titles.
- **Sentence case:** Capitalizes only the first letter of each sentence and proper nouns (`The quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog.`). Modern product design systems and UI/UX guidelines (such as Apple Human Interface Guidelines and Google Material Design 3) recommend sentence case for button labels, navigation menus, and form placeholders to improve scanning speed and user comprehension.

### 2. Software Engineering and Programming Conventions
Software programming languages enforce strict naming conventions for variables, classes, database tables, and network endpoints:

- **camelCase (lowerCamelCase):** The initial word begins with a lowercase letter, and each subsequent compound word begins with an uppercase letter without spaces (`userAccountBalance`). Standard convention for variables, object properties, and functions in JavaScript, TypeScript, Java, Swift, Kotlin, and Go.
- **PascalCase (UpperCamelCase):** Every compound word begins with a capital letter (`UserAccountBalance`). Mandated for classes, interfaces, React/Vue components, and type definitions across C#, TypeScript, Java, and Python.
- **snake_case:** All words are in lowercase, separated by an underscore (`user_account_balance`). The standard convention for variable and function names in Python (PEP 8), Ruby, Rust, C, and for relational database columns and tables across PostgreSQL and MySQL.
- **SCREAMING_SNAKE_CASE:** Every word is in uppercase, separated by an underscore (`MAX_RETRY_ATTEMPTS`). Reserved for global constants, immutable application configurations, and system environment variables (`.env`).
- **kebab-case (hyphen-case or slug):** All words are in lowercase, separated by a hyphen (`user-account-balance`). Standard for CSS classes, HTML data attributes, URL slugs, REST API endpoint paths, and Kubernetes YAML configurations.
- **Train-Case (HTTP-Header-Case):** Compound words with capitalized initials separated by hyphens (`Content-Type`, `X-Forwarded-For`). Universal standard for HTTP request and response network headers.

## Editorial Title Case Rules: AP vs Chicago vs APA

Converting text to proper Title Case involves subtle linguistic rules regarding which words should remain lowercase. Major editorial style guides agree on basic principles but diverge on specific edge cases:

### Words That Remain Lowercase in Title Case
1. **Articles:** `a`, `an`, `the` (unless they appear as the first or last word of the title).
2. **Short Prepositions:** `in`, `on`, `at`, `by`, `for`, `with`, `to`, `of`, `off`, `up`, `via`.
3. **Coordinating Conjunctions:** `and`, `but`, `for`, `or`, `nor`, `so`, `yet`.

### Differences Between Major Style Guides
- **The Associated Press (AP Stylebook):** Capitalizes all words of four or more letters, regardless of part of speech. Prepositions like `with`, `into`, and `from` are capitalized because they contain four letters.
- **The Chicago Manual of Style (CMOS):** Lowercases all prepositions regardless of length (e.g., `between`, `through`, `without`), unless they function as adverbs or adjectives in phrasal verbs.
- **APA Style (American Psychological Association):** Capitalizes all major words and words of four letters or more, including prepositions.

## Internationalization and Unicode Casing Nuances

Case transformation is not purely a mathematical subtraction of ASCII character codes (`char - 32`). In international computing, Unicode casing involves language-specific linguistic rules:

- **The Turkish Dotless and Dotted I:** In Turkish and Azerbaijani, the letter `I` has two distinct pairs: the dotted `i` capitalizes to `İ` (`U+0130`), while the dotless `ı` (`U+0131`) capitalizes to `I`. In standard English algorithms, lowercase `i` erroneously capitalizes to `I`, altering the lexical meaning in Turkish text.
- **The German Sharp S (Eszett / `ß`):** Historically, `ß` capitalized to double `SS` (`Straße` becomes `STRASSE`). In 2017, the Council for German Orthography officially introduced the capital sharp S (`ẞ`, `U+1E9E`).
- **The Greek Final Sigma:** In Greek, uppercase `Σ` has two lowercase variants: `σ` in the middle of words and `ς` (final sigma) at the end of words.
