## How much is a 20% tip on a $60 bill?

A 20% tip on $60 is $12. With no additional tax, the total is $72, or $24 each for three people. If the $60 is the subtotal and the receipt adds $4.80 tax, enter $60 and 8% tax. Tipping on the subtotal still gives $12. The three guests now owe $25.60 each.

If you enter the tax-inclusive total from a receipt, leave the tax field at zero to avoid counting the tax twice.

## Is the suggested tip on my receipt before or after sales tax?

On a US restaurant receipt, the suggested tip amounts can help you check which total the restaurant used. Suppose the food subtotal is $50 and sales tax is $4. A suggested 20% tip of $10 uses the subtotal. A suggestion of $10.80 uses the $54 after-tax total. If you leave $10, the final bill is $64, or $32 each for two diners.

## The Mathematics of Tipping: Formulas and Step-by-Step Math

Tipping calculations follow straightforward mathematical formulas, though subtle factors like taxes and rounding affect final out-of-pocket expenses:

### 1. Gratuity and Grand Total Formulas
To calculate the total tip based on the pre-tax food and beverage subtotal:

$$\text{Tip Amount} = \text{Subtotal} \times \left(\frac{\text{Tip Percentage}}{100}\right)$$

Adding the gratuity to the food and beverage subtotal and any applicable sales tax yields the grand dining total:

$$\text{Grand Total} = \text{Subtotal} + \text{Sales Tax} + \text{Tip Amount}$$

### 2. Fair Bill Splitting per Guest
To divide the tab equally among dining companions:

$$\text{Cost per Person} = \frac{\text{Grand Total}}{\text{Number of Guests}} = \frac{\text{Subtotal} + \text{Sales Tax} + \text{Tip Amount}}{\text{Number of Guests}}$$

### 3. Worked Real-World Examples

- **Example 1 (Casual Lunch for 2):** Two colleagues have lunch. The food subtotal is $\$44.00$, local sales tax is $\$3.52$ ($8\%$), and they choose an $18\%$ tip:
  - Total Gratuity: $\$44.00 \times 0.18 = \$7.92$
  - Grand Total: $\$44.00 + \$3.52 + \$7.92 = \$55.44$
  - Per-Person Share: $\$55.44 \div 2 = \$27.72$ per person (each person contributes $\$23.76$ toward food/tax and $\$3.96$ toward tip).

- **Example 2 (Dinner Party for 4 with Rounding):** Four friends dine out. The food subtotal is $\$140.00$, with no separate tax, and a $20\%$ tip:
  - Total Gratuity: $\$140.00 \times 0.20 = \$28.00$
  - Grand Total: $\$140.00 + \$28.00 = \$168.00$
  - Per-Person Share: $\$168.00 \div 4 = \$42.00$ per person exact.

## Pre-Tax vs Post-Tax Tipping: What Do Etiquette Experts Recommend?

One of the most common dilemmas at the dining table is whether gratuity should be calculated on the pre-tax food subtotal or the post-tax grand total:

- **The Pre-Tax Standard (Etiquette Guideline):** Leading etiquette authorities (including the Emily Post Institute) and hospitality associations recommend calculating gratuity based strictly on the **pre-tax subtotal** of food and beverage services. Sales tax is an obligatory government assessment, not a personal hospitality service rendered by your server.
- **The Post-Tax Reality (POS Terminal Presets):** Modern digital payment terminals (such as Toast, Clover, and Square) frequently calculate their suggested preset tip percentages ($18\%$, $20\%$, $25\%$) against the post-tax total, which includes state sales taxes, local hospitality surcharges, and alcohol levies. On large dining bills in high-tax metropolitan areas (where sales tax can exceed $10\%$), tipping on post-tax totals adds an extra $2\%$ to $3\%$ to your effective tip rate.

## What percentage did I tip?

Divide the tip you left by the bill amount you used as the tipping base, then multiply by 100. If your receipt shows a $48 subtotal and you left $9, your tip was 18.75%. If the receipt lists only the final payment and the bill total, subtract the bill total from the payment first to find the tip.

For a different receipt, enter the tip as the part and the tipping base as the total in the [percentage calculator](/percentage-calculator/).

## Group Bill Splitting: Equal Division vs Itemized Separation

When dining with friends, colleagues, or family, splitting the check can sometimes create tension. Keep these guidelines in mind:

1. **Equal Splits:** If everyone ordered entrees of similar price and shared appetizers, dividing the bill equally is the most socially seamless approach.
2. **Itemized Splits for Unequal Orders:** If certain guests ordered expensive alcoholic drinks, premium steaks, or multiple desserts while others had a side salad and tap water, separate itemized checks should be requested from the server, or the heavier spenders should voluntarily contribute extra toward the final bill and gratuity.
3. **Rounding Up for Mobile Payments:** When settling group expenses on Venmo, Zelle, or Cash App, rounding up per-person shares to the nearest whole dollar eliminates awkward cent transfers and leaves an extra dollar of appreciation for your server.

## Automatic Gratuity for Large Parties: Avoid Double Tipping

When dining with groups of 5, 6, 8, or more guests, many restaurants automatically add an **automatic gratuity** (typically $18\%$ or $20\%$, labeled on the receipt as *"Auto-Gratuity"*, *"Service Charge"*, or *"Large Party Fee"*). 

Always examine the printed merchant slip before writing a tip on the "Additional Tip" line. If the automatic gratuity line has already been added and totaled into the final balance, adding another $20\%$ results in double-tipping (a $40\%$ total tip).

## 3 Fast Mental Math Tipping Tricks

If you need to calculate gratuity quickly without opening an app, these three mental arithmetic shortcuts work every time:

1. **Find 10% Instantly:** Move the decimal point one position to the left. On an $\$80.00$ meal, $10\%$ is $\$8.00$.
2. **Double for 20%:** Take your $10\%$ number and multiply by 2 ($\$8.00 \times 2 = \$16.00$).
3. **Add Half for 15%:** Take your $10\%$ figure ($\$8.00$), add half of that amount ($\$4.00$), giving $\$12.00$ total tip.
