## The Mathematics of Retail Discounts: Formulas and Step-by-Step Math

Understanding how discounts and promotional markdowns are computed prevents checkout surprises and helps evaluate retail claims:

### 1. Basic Percentage Discount Formula
To calculate the total money saved on an item:

$$\text{Savings} = \text{Original Price} \times \left(\frac{\text{Discount Percentage}}{100}\right)$$

Subtracting savings from the original price yields the discounted subtotal:

$$\text{Discounted Price} = \text{Original Price} - \text{Savings} = \text{Original Price} \times \left(1 - \frac{\text{Discount Percentage}}{100}\right)$$

### 2. Factoring in Sales Tax
In jurisdictions where sales tax is levied at the cash register (such as the United States and Canada), tax is calculated on the discounted sale price rather than the original sticker price:

$$\text{Tax Amount} = \text{Discounted Price} \times \left(\frac{\text{Tax Rate Percentage}}{100}\right)$$

$$\text{Final Out-of-Pocket Total} = \text{Discounted Price} + \text{Tax Amount}$$

*Worked Example:* A winter coat retails for $\$150.00$ with a $30\%$ promotional discount in a city with an $8.5\%$ sales tax rate:
- Monetary Savings: $\$150.00 \times 0.30 = \$45.00$
- Discounted Subtotal: $\$150.00 - \$45.00 = \$105.00$
- Sales Tax: $\$105.00 \times 0.085 = \$8.925 \approx \$8.93$
- Final Total: $\$105.00 + \$8.93 = \$113.93$

## Double Discounts: The Truth Behind Stacked Percent Off Promotions

Retailers frequently advertise promotions such as *"Take 40% off clearance items, plus an extra 20% off at the register."* Many shoppers mistakenly add the two percentages together ($40\% + 20\% = 60\%$). In retail reality, stacked discounts are applied sequentially:

$$\text{Effective Multiplier} = (1 - D_1) \times (1 - D_2)$$

Applying a $40\%$ discount followed by a $20\%$ discount yields:

$$\text{Effective Multiplier} = (1 - 0.40) \times (1 - 0.20) = 0.60 \times 0.80 = 0.48$$

An effective price multiplier of $0.48$ equates to a **$52\%$ total discount**, not $60\%$. On a $\$100.00$ item:
- First Markdown ($40\%$ off): $\$100.00 - \$40.00 = \$60.00$
- Second Markdown ($20\%$ off remaining $\$60$): $\$60.00 - \$12.00 = \$48.00$
- Total Out-of-Pocket Savings: $\$52.00$ ($52\%$ net savings).

## Evaluating BOGO (Buy One, Get One) Promotions

Retail stores frequently structure markdowns using promotional bundling. Here is the mathematical reality behind common BOGO deals:

- **Buy One, Get One Free (BOGO Free):** Purchasing two identical items yields an effective **$50\%$ discount** per unit. If one item is cheaper, the discount is strictly less than $50\%$.
- **Buy One, Get One 50% Off (BOGO 50%):** You pay $100\%$ for the first item and $50\%$ for the second item ($150\%$ total cost for 2 units). The true effective discount across both units is **$25\%$ per item**.
- **Buy Two, Get One Free (Buy 2 Get 1):** You purchase 3 items for the price of 2. The effective markdown is $\frac{1}{3} = \mathbf{33.33\%}$ per item.

## Reverse Discount Calculation: Finding the Original Sticker Price

If you know the final sale price and the discount percentage that was applied, you can reverse the equation to find the original pre-sale sticker price:

$$\text{Original Price} = \frac{\text{Sale Price}}{1 - \left(\frac{\text{Discount Percentage}}{100}\right)}$$

*Example:* A clearance jacket was purchased for $\$64.00$ after a $60\%$ markdown:

$$\text{Original Price} = \frac{\$64.00}{1 - 0.60} = \frac{\$64.00}{0.40} = \$160.00$$

This formula is indispensable for auditing store receipts, verifying price tracking histories, and calculating insurance replacement valuations.
