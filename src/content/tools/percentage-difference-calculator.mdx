Use our free **percentage difference calculator** to compare the relative difference between two positive numbers. Unlike percentage change—which requires a chronological before-and-after sequence—percentage difference compares two independent values (such as test scores between two students, product prices at competing retailers, or output metrics between two factory shifts) by evaluating their difference against their average.

## What is percentage difference?

Percentage difference expresses the absolute variation between two numerical measurements as a percentage of their arithmetic mean (average). 

Because neither number is designated as the "original" or "starting" baseline, swapping the order of the two numbers produces the exact same percentage outcome. Furthermore, because the formula uses the absolute difference ($|A - B|$), the resulting percentage is **always positive**.

## The percentage difference formula

The standard formula for calculating percentage difference between value $A$ and value $B$ is:

$$\text{Percentage Difference} = \left(\frac{|A - B|}{\frac{A + B}{2}}\right) \times 100$$

### Step-by-Step Worked Example:

Suppose two electronics stores sell identical wireless headphones. Store A charges $120.00 and Store B charges $150.00:

1. **Calculate the Absolute Difference:** $|120 - 150| = |-30| = 30$
2. **Calculate the Average (Midpoint):** $\frac{120 + 150}{2} = \frac{270}{2} = 135$
3. **Divide Difference by Average:** $\frac{30}{135} \approx 0.2222$
4. **Multiply by 100:** $0.2222 \times 100 = 22.22\%$

The percentage difference between the two store prices is **22.22%**.

## How percentage difference differs from percentage change

Understanding when to apply percentage difference versus percentage change prevents analytical errors:

| Comparison Criterion | Percentage Difference | Percentage Change / Increase |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Order of Inputs** | Order does not matter ($A$ and $B$ are interchangeable) | Order is critical (must designate Old and New) |
| **Denominator Used** | Average of the two values: $\frac{A + B}{2}$ | Starting original baseline value: $\text{Old}$ |
| **Sign of Result** | Always positive ($\ge 0\%$) | Can be positive (increase) or negative (decrease) |
| **Typical Use Cases** | Competitor price comparison, experimental variance, lab trials | Financial returns, inflation, weight loss, sales growth |
